Airchain — Broadcast Ingest & Delivery Automation

It started with one slow task. Syndicated shows send promos with none of our branding, and each one needs a station tag mixed in with that show's air times. At five promos a show across 10+ shows, that's about 50 files to edit every week. My first fix was an Adobe Audition Favorite I could batch-run, but I still had to check every file, fix the odd ones by hand, and drop them into the radio software myself.

So I built Airchain, a Windows desktop app made end-to-end with Claude Code. It now handles the whole pipeline, from syndicated show files to the on-air automation system:

  • Watches local folders or polls FTP/SFTP servers for syndicated shows, with a visual filename-pattern builder
  • A configurable audio chain (trim, EQ, compression, EBU R128 loudness) and BWF/CART metadata written to every file
  • Hold & release — files are delivered to the automation system at the right time before airdate
  • Promo handling with auto-tagging and a waveform editor for one-click approval
  • Mission Control calendar, to-do cards for missing or stuck shows, and a diagnose panel that explains why an episode didn't complete
  • A weather recorder that builds scripts from NWS forecasts, plus proof-of-airing reports and invoicing

Now: I open the queue that built up while I was out (often days ahead), listen, and approve. Several files take under a minute, and the most consistent promos don't need my approval at all. Airchain holds them and drops them into the automation system right when they're scheduled to air.

CheckSpree — Open-Source Check Printing & Ledger

A free, open-source tool I built and shipped publicly. It started as a simple way to fill in a check digitally and print it onto check paper, and grew into a full ledger:

  • Digital check entry that prints cleanly onto pre-loaded check stock
  • A ledger that tracks every check, keeps running balances, and auto-prefills from saved payee profiles
  • Free for anyone to use and extend

Actively developed — the core is solid, with a few features still being hardened.
